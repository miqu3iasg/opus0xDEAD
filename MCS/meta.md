# CS10326 — Mathematics for Computer Science

## Overview

**Authors:** Eric Lehman, F. Thomson Leighton, Albert R. Meyer
**Edition:** 2015
**Subject:** Discrete Mathematics, Probability, and Mathematical Foundations of Computer Science

_Mathematics for Computer Science_ develops the mathematical foundations needed for computer science, with an emphasis on rigorous reasoning rather than calculation alone.

The book covers mathematical proofs, induction, number theory, graph theory, relations, counting, asymptotics, recurrences, infinite sets, probability, random variables, expectation, deviations, and random walks.

The material is based on MIT's undergraduate course _Mathematics for Computer Science (6.042J)_.

## Learning Objectives

By completing this book, I should be able to:

- Read and construct rigorous mathematical proofs
- Work with propositional and predicate logic.
- Apply direct proof, contradiction, cases, and induction.
- Reason about integers, divisibility, modular arithmetic, and number theory.
- Model and analyze problems using graphs and directed graphs.
- Work with relations, partial orders, and state machines.
- Analyze sums, asymptotic growth, and recurrence relations.
- Solve counting problems using combinatorial techniques.
- Understand infinite sets and cardinality.
- Construct and use generating functions.
- Model uncertainty using probability spaces and conditional probability.
- Reason about independence and random variables.
- Calculate and interpret expectation and variance.
- Analyze deviations from expected values.
- Understand random walks and their mathematical properties.
